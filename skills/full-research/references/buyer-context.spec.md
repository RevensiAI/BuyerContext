# Buyer context specification

The canonical output is `buyer-context.md`. It is a decision document, not a transcript dump. Keep it concise enough to guide product, go-to-market, content, sales, and AI agents while retaining citations for important claims.

Use this structure:

```markdown
# Buyer context

Last updated: YYYY-MM-DD
Research scope: ...
Overall confidence: high | medium | low

## Executive summary
Who buys, the progress they seek, why they choose this offer, and the most important qualification boundary.

## Company and offer
- What the company provides
- How value is delivered
- Commercial model, if known
- Important constraints

## Ideal customer profile
- Best-fit segments
- Company characteristics
- Relevant maturity or situation
- Geography, if material
- Anti-ICP / poor-fit conditions

## Buying committee
| Role | Goals | Concerns | Influence | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Jobs to be done
- Primary job
- Supporting functional, emotional, and social jobs

## Trigger events
- Events or changes that create urgency

## Pains and stakes
- Current friction
- Business and personal consequences of inaction

## Desired outcomes
- Observable outcomes buyers value
- Success measures, where supported

## Decision criteria
- Requirements and trade-offs used to evaluate options

## Objections and perceived risks
- Adoption, switching, delivery, security, commercial, and career risks

## Alternatives and competitive frame
- Direct competitors
- Internal, manual, incumbent, or do-nothing alternatives
- Why buyers choose or reject each path

## Buyer language
- Verbatim phrases with source IDs
- Terms buyers use and terms they avoid

## Proof buyers trust
- Evidence that reduces risk: outcomes, references, demonstrations, expertise, or guarantees

## Positioning implications
- One-line positioning
- Core claim
- Defensible differentiators
- Messages to avoid

## Unknowns and research priorities
- Important unanswered questions
- Conflicting evidence
- Next evidence to collect

## Sources
- [C01] ...
```

## Rules

- Mark unsupported sections `Unknown`; do not fill gaps with generic personas.
- Cite material claims with source IDs. Give major conclusions a confidence label when confidence is not obvious.
- Keep distinct segments separate when their jobs, criteria, or buying process differ materially.
- Include only buyer details that could change a real decision.
- Retain useful contradictions and minority views.
- When refreshing an existing file, preserve still-supported knowledge and summarize meaningful changes.
