# Customer Research

Create `research/customers.md` from the customer evidence the user places in scope. This is usually the highest-value input to buyer context.

## Method

1. Read `references/research-method.md`.
2. Inventory the supplied sources before drawing conclusions. Record source type, date, customer segment, buying stage, and whether the outcome was a win, loss, churn, or unknown when available.
3. Read a representative set. If the corpus is too large to review fully, explain the sampling method and its limitations.
4. Code evidence for jobs, trigger events, pains, desired outcomes, decision criteria, objections, perceived risks, alternatives, buying roles, proof, and verbatim language.
5. Compare patterns by meaningful segment and outcome. Retain disconfirming cases and distinguish mention frequency from business importance.
6. Write `research/customers.md` with:
   - corpus and method;
   - segment and buying-stage coverage;
   - strongest findings with confidence and source IDs;
   - jobs, triggers, pains, outcomes, criteria, objections, alternatives, roles, and proof;
   - buyer-language excerpts;
   - differences by segment or outcome;
   - contradictions and unknowns;
   - recommended follow-up research;
   - source register.
7. Summarize what should change in `buyer-context.md`.

## Privacy and accuracy

- Read only files the user has placed in scope.
- Keep private customer evidence local. Do not put its contents into web searches or external research services.
- Prefer anonymized source IDs and roles over personal names.
- Never invent, clean up, or merge quotes. Use ellipses only when they do not change meaning.
- Avoid claiming statistical prevalence from a qualitative or incomplete sample.
